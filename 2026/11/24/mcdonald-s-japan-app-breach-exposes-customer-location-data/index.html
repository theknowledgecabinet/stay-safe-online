McDonald's Japan app breach exposes customer location data

McDonald's Japan confirmed a data breach affecting its app users, revealing purchase histories and location details, though no names or credit card numbers were stolen.

Tuesday 24 November 2026, 8.00pm

What happened

McDonald's Japan has confirmed that attackers accessed a database linked to its official smartphone app. The company said the exposed information includes customers' product purchase history, IP addresses, and mobile device location data.

The fast-food chain stated that names, email addresses, phone numbers, home addresses, and credit card details were not part of the breach. It also noted that the internal customer IDs exposed cannot be used on their own to identify specific individuals.

McDonald's Japan said it is working to invalidate the access credentials used in the attack and has reported the incident to Japan's data protection authorities. The company added that it has not detected any misuse of the app or services as a result of the incident.

This breach follows a wave of cyberattacks on Japanese companies this year. Last month, Burger King Japan also disclosed a major data breach affecting millions of customer records.

Am I affected?

The sources say the breach affects users of the McDonald's Japan official smartphone app. If you use this app, check your account for any unusual activity. The company said customers do not need to change passwords or take other security measures.

What to do

  • Check your bank statements for any unrecognised transactions, as a general precaution.
  • Keep your phone and app updated to the latest version.
  • Be wary of phishing emails or messages claiming to be from McDonald's asking for your details.
