South Korea power firm KEPCO confirms breach of 1.5 million records

A major South Korean electricity company says its systems were hacked, exposing the personal and financial details of over 1.5 million people.

Tuesday 24 November 2026, 6.17am

What happened

Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO) has confirmed that an unauthorised user accessed its internal network. The company detected the breach on 20 November 2026. It quickly blocked outside access and suspended the affected services to stop further damage.

The breach affected three groups of people. Details for 650,487 customers were exposed, including names, addresses, bank account numbers, and card details. Information for 426,827 company representatives and 442,934 employees was also accessed. This included names, ID information, and contact details.

KEPCO says the attack used "advanced technologies such as AI" but did not give specific details on how the hacker got in. A threat actor using the alias "Noiem" has claimed responsibility on a hacker forum. However, authorities have not yet confirmed this attribution. The company is working with government and financial bodies to prevent misuse of the data.

The company is notifying affected individuals directly. It has also filed a report with data protection authorities. KEPCO warns that the exposed data could be used for targeted phishing. Phishing involves fake messages that try to trick you into giving away your details.

Am I affected?

The sources say the breach affects customers, employees, and contractors of KEPCO in South Korea. The sources don't say if UK residents are affected. You can check if you are affected by looking for direct notifications from the company or checking your bank statements for unusual activity.

What to do

  • Be wary of unexpected calls or messages asking for personal details or verification codes.
  • Do not install unknown apps or enter information into suspicious online forms.
  • Report any attempts to scam you to the police or relevant security agencies.
