AI service providers found to have had customer prompts stolen

Security firm Dragos says evidence indicates that at least three AI services appear to have been hacked, with customer prompts stolen without the companies knowing.

Tuesday 24 November 2026, 6.18am

What happened

Cybersecurity firm Dragos says it discovered that at least three AI inference providers were breached. These companies host AI models and sell access to them. Dragos claims the intrusions were not detected until the firm notified the providers.

To find the leaks, Dragos sent about two million test messages to various AI services between July and November. These messages contained unique, trackable bait, such as fake web addresses and security keys. The firm then monitored the internet to see if anyone used this bait, which would prove the data had been stolen.

Dragos found evidence that attackers stole information from one of the tested providers. The firm’s chief research officer, HD Moore, said that because the test messages were processed alongside real customer requests, those real requests were “almost certainly copied too.” The biggest breach involved roughly 200 test keys stolen in late October.

OpenRouter, a service that connects users to many AI providers, said its own systems were not breached. However, it removed three unnamed providers in response to the incident. Other affected companies include Phala, Together AI, Novita, and Atlas Cloud.

Am I affected?

The sources identify Phala, Together AI, Novita, and Atlas Cloud as affected. You should check your email for any security notices from the AI services you use. You can also look at your bank statements for any unusual activity, though this story does not mention financial theft.

What to do

  • Check your email for any security notices from AI services you use.
  • Review your bank statements for any unusual activity.
  • If you use AI services, check their official websites for updates on data security.
