US lawmakers warn ID check firms after massive data leak

A group of US politicians has written to identity verification companies after a breach exposed millions of ID scans, raising fears about how your personal details are stored.

Tuesday 24 November 2026, 8.06pm

What happened

A bipartisan group of US federal lawmakers sent a letter to several identity verification companies on Monday. They are asking these firms to explain how they protect sensitive customer data. The letter cites a breach at a company called IDScan that happened in September.

According to the letter, the breach led to scans of more than 150 million people’s drivers’ licenses and other ID documents appearing for sale on the dark web. The dark web is a hidden part of the internet where illegal goods are often traded. The stolen data included detailed images, such as ultraviolet and infrared scans, which make the documents harder to fake.

The lawmakers said the breach also exposed nearly 600,000 medical cards and employment authorisations. They warned that foreign adversaries could use this commercial data to infer the behaviour of Americans. The letter urged companies to limit how much personal information they collect and store to reduce the risk of future hacks.

Independent journalist Brian Krebs, who first reported the breach, said he has found more than two dozen identity verification companies that store scanned IDs by default. He noted that customers often have to pay extra to stop these scans from being kept after the check is done.

Am I affected?

The sources say the breach affected Canadian and US victims, including high-ranking military and intelligence officials. The sources do not say if UK residents are affected. You should check your bank statements and email for any unusual activity or notices from services you use.

What to do

  • Check your email for any official notices from services you use that verify your identity.
  • Review your bank statements for any unrecognised transactions.
  • Be cautious of phishing - fake messages that try to trick you into giving away details - especially if they claim to be from ID verification services.
