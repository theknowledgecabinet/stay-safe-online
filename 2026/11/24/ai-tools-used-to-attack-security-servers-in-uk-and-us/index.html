AI tools used to attack security servers in UK and US

A criminal group is using AI tools to attack security servers used by major UK and global organisations.

Tuesday 24 November 2026, 8.19pm

What happened

Security firm GreyNoise says a criminal group is using AI-powered tools to attack Check Point Security Management servers. These servers are used by large organisations to manage their network security.

The group, tracked as GT13, targeted at least 22 organisations in the UK, US, and other countries. The sectors include energy, finance, government, and manufacturing. GreyNoise says the campaign started in early October and may still be ongoing.

The attackers used AI to find weak points in systems and exploit them automatically. They also used manual methods to gain control. GreyNoise says the group accessed servers from 99 different internet addresses, many of which were temporary or compromised devices.

The attackers stole data and moved between different systems. They used one victim’s server as a base to send commands to other compromised servers. GreyNoise confirmed activity between 17 October and 2 November, but believes the threat continues.

Am I affected?

The sources say the targets are large organisations in sectors like energy, finance, and government, not individual homes. The sources don't say who is affected. If you work for a large company, check your internal security notices. If you are a consumer, this story is about business systems, so your personal accounts are likely not directly targeted by this specific campaign.

What to do

  • Keep your devices and software up to date to protect against known weaknesses.
  • Use strong, unique passwords for your work and personal accounts.
  • Be careful with unexpected emails or messages that ask for your details.
  • Check your bank statements regularly for any unusual activity.
