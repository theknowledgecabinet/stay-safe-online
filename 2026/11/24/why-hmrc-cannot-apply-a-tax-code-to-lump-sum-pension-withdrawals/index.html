Why HMRC cannot apply a tax code to lump sum pension withdrawals

People taking one-off pension payments may pay tax upfront because HMRC struggles with ad-hoc withdrawals and cannot apply a standard tax code.

Tuesday 24 November 2026, 8.59pm

What happened

HMRC can send a tax code to pension providers for regular monthly payments. You may pay too much tax on your first payment, but once a tax code is issued, HMRC will refund you the following month. However, this process does not work for one-off lump sum withdrawals.

According to Which? expert Joanne Padilla, HMRC struggles with ad-hoc withdrawals. As a result, these payments are often taxed using a higher-rate emergency tax code. This means you may pay more tax than you actually owe.

You do not have to wait until the end of the tax year to get your money back. You can reclaim the overpaid tax directly from HMRC at any time.

HMRC says you should receive a refund of your overpaid tax within 30 days of submitting your claim.

Am I affected?

This affects people who take one-off lump sum withdrawals from a drawdown pension pot. If you take regular monthly income, HMRC can apply a tax code, so you are likely not affected in the same way.

What to do

  • Complete one of the three forms to reclaim overpaid tax: P55, P53Z, or P50Z.
  • Submit the form online or by post to HMRC.
  • Wait up to 30 days for HMRC to process your refund.
