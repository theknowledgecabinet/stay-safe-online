Donation platform breach exposes donor details

A cyberattack on a fundraising software provider leaked personal details of donors to the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace.

Tuesday 24 November 2026, 8.17pm

What happened

The Carnegie Endowment for International Peace says some donors had their information leaked due to a cyberattack on a third-party vendor called Soapbox Engage. The organisation sent emails to past donors on Friday to warn them about the breach. It said the incident happened on 28 October and that the attackers were removed from the system on 30 October.

Soapbox Engage is an online fundraising platform used by charities. The Carnegie Endowment uses it for online donations. According to the organisation, the hackers stole names, email addresses, phone numbers and giving history. A spokesperson said no full credit card numbers were exposed.

The Carnegie Endowment hired a cybersecurity firm to confirm the attackers were removed. The organisation notified everyone in its database because of European data privacy laws. It did not say how many people were affected.

This incident happened at the same time as other fundraising platforms reported security issues. GoFundMe said it identified a cybersecurity incident where hackers breached its systems in two separate attacks in October and November. It said it has disabled unauthorised accounts and is monitoring for further suspicious activity.

Am I affected?

The sources say donors to the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace were affected. You can check if you are affected by looking for an email warning from the organisation. You should also check your bank statements for any unrecognised transactions.

What to do

  • Check your email for a warning from the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace.
  • Review your bank and credit card statements for any unauthorised charges.
  • Be careful of phishing - fake messages that try to trick you into giving away details - especially if they ask for your login information.
