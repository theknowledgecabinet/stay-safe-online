FIFA confirms hackers accessed personal and business data

FIFA has confirmed a data breach, but a hacking group's claim that it released 12.2 GB of files is unverified.

Thursday 26 November 2026, 3.56pm

What happened

FIFA, the world football governing body based in Switzerland, has confirmed that hackers gained unauthorised access to a limited set of personal and business information. A spokesperson told The Record on 25 November that the organisation is working with law enforcement and other authorities.

A group called the Handsome Boy Hacking Club (HBHC) claims to have stolen and published 12.2 GB of sensitive internal documents. However, FIFA has not confirmed this claim or the size of the data involved. Dark Web Informer, which reported the group's post on X, noted that the claim is unverified.

The group says the files include identity documents, executive information, and financial records. It also warned that some folders may contain corrupted or duplicate data. This means the total file size is a poor measure of how many people were actually affected.

FIFA has not said how the hackers got in, when the intrusion was detected, or whose specific information was taken. The group mentioned a 'Microsoft environment', but this does not confirm that a specific Microsoft product or flaw was involved.

Am I affected?

The sources do not say who is affected. FIFA has not named whose records were involved.

What to do

  • Verify any unexpected messages asking you to upload identity documents through an official channel before sharing anything.
  • Be wary of fake websites that may try to collect personal information in the name of the breach.
