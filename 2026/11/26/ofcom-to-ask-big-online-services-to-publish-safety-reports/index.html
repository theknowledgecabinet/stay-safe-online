Ofcom to ask big online services to publish safety reports

Ofcom is consulting on rules that will force major social media and search sites to publish annual reports on how they keep users safe.

Thursday 26 November 2026, 3.55pm

What happened

Under the Online Safety Act, providers of widely used services like social media, search engines, and pornography sites must publish annual transparency reports. These reports will show how these services protect users, particularly children, from harm.

Ofcom has launched a consultation to decide what information these reports should include. They want to ensure the data helps improve services and empowers users. For example, reports may cover how services handle user complaints or address risky features.

When Ofcom creates a report request, or 'transparency notice', for a service, it considers factors like the type of service and how many children use it. Ofcom has already spoken with civil society groups and experts to inform this approach.

Providers will have the chance to see draft notices before final ones are sent next year. Ofcom will also produce its own annual report to compare services and highlight good or poor practice across the industry.

Am I affected?

The sources say this applies to providers of widely used online services, including social media, search, and pornography services. The information published in these reports is intended to help make services safer and empower the people who use them. The sources do not specify which individual companies are currently in scope.

What to do

  • Check if your favourite social media or search apps publish annual transparency reports.
  • Look for information on how these services handle user complaints and protect children.
  • Review Ofcom’s own annual report to see how different services compare on safety.
