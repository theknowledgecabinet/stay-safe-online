Scams and fraud

Holiday shopping scams nearly double, says McAfee report

A new survey shows more people are falling for fake deals, with many losing money and having to cut back on essentials.

Thursday 26 November 2026, 6.12am

What happened

McAfee says the share of Americans who fell for a holiday shopping scam has nearly doubled since last year. Forty-two per cent said they were victims, up from 22 per cent in 2025. The company notes that shoppers are now less confident in their ability to spot these tricks.

Scammers are using popular gifts to lure shoppers to lookalike websites. McAfee linked 12 popular products, such as specific sneakers and trading cards, to suspicious online activity. The company warns that these are fake sites, not problems with the real brands or retailers.

Many people said they saw fake deals for well-known shops like Amazon and Temu. Scammers often use phishing, which means sending fake messages that try to trick you into giving away details or clicking dangerous links. Positive reviews on a fake site can also make it look genuine.

The financial impact is serious. Of those who lost money, 60 per cent lost $100 or more. Nearly half recovered none of their money. Some victims had to borrow money or cut back on everyday essentials to cover the loss.

Am I affected?

The sources say this survey focused on Americans, so it does not confirm if UK readers are affected. However, the types of scams described, such as fake deals for popular gifts, are common everywhere. You can check your own bank statements and emails for any unexpected charges or messages about deliveries.

What to do

  • Be careful with deals that seem too good to be true, especially if the price is unusually low.
  • Check the website address carefully to make sure it is the official shop, not a lookalike.
  • Do not click links in messages about package delivery problems unless you are sure they are genuine.
  • If you think you have been scammed, contact your bank immediately to try to recover your money.
