FCA warns financial firms to control AI risks or face action

The UK's financial regulator has told financial firms they must address cybersecurity risks from the deployment of AI agents, or face regulatory action.

Thursday 26 November 2026, 4.00pm

What happened

The Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) has issued new guidance to financial firms. It warns that AI agents are being widely deployed in the financial services sector to improve productivity and convenience to customers. The regulator says these products can blur the boundary between regulated and unregulated activity, and the protections consumers receive in relation to their finances and data.

Banks are now using these AI agents to initiate transactions, move money and make investment decisions. The FCA notes that without appropriate safeguards, such as strict access controls, agents have been observed taking dangerous actions such as gaining access to sensitive systems and data, even in pursuit of legitimate aims. Senior managers responsible for AI models deployed in a specific area of the business need to take “reasonable steps” to understand the risks arising from AI. This includes providing effective oversight and ensuring governance controls and escalation arrangements are appropriate for the risks.

Regulated financial organisations should consider any liabilities from accepting agent instructions and whether it is appropriate to accept payments instructed by an AI agent. They should also help consumers understand the limits of financial information and advice generated by their models through clear, proportionate and consistent disclaimers. Responsibility for complying with regulatory obligations remains with senior managers and the organisation as a whole, whether the firm deploys agentic AI themselves or relies on a third-party provider.

The FCA will assess the services firms provide through agentic products, including how they develop over time, to ensure they keep to regulatory requirements. Failure to adhere to existing regulatory requirements, including data protection laws, will see the FCA use its powers to sanction non-compliant organisations. These powers include imposing financial penalties, prohibiting individuals from carrying out regulated activities, public censure and prosecution. The FCA also offers support services, including testing whether these tools meet regulatory requirements.

Am I affected?

The guidance applies to regulated financial organisations. If you use a bank or investment service that uses AI to handle your money, you are affected. Check your bank's website or app for notices about how they use AI tools.

What to do

  • Check your bank's privacy and security pages to see if they use AI agents for transactions.
  • Look for clear disclaimers in your app or website that explain the limits of AI-generated advice.
  • Review your bank statements regularly to spot any unusual transactions you did not authorise.
  • Contact your bank directly if you are unsure about how an AI tool is handling your money.
