UK data watchdog lifts staff silence orders

The UK data watchdog is lifting silence orders so staff can speak freely in an independent review of its workplace culture.

Thursday 26 November 2026, 4.01pm

What happened

The Information Commissioner’s Office (ICO) will allow current and former staff to share their experiences in an independent review. This follows the resignation of the information commissioner, John Edwards, in June after allegations of sexual harassment and bullying.

Ministers expect to announce details of the review this week. The review will look at the culture, accountability, and governance at the organisation. Staff had previously signed non-disclosure agreements (NDAs), which are contracts that stop people from talking about their time at work.

According to freedom of information disclosures, the ICO signed 16 such agreements since January 2020. Nine of these included clauses to stop employees making negative comments about their former employer. A union representative said lifting these restrictions is necessary to get the full truth.

The ICO said it is committed to learning from the past. It confirmed it will not use these agreements to stop people from participating in the review. The organisation has also restructured its leadership, moving decision-making power to a board rather than a single commissioner.

Am I affected?

The sources do not say who is affected. You can check your account emails, bank statements, or the service's own notices for updates.

What to do

  • Keep an eye on official news channels for updates on the independent review.
  • Review your own workplace contracts if you are concerned about non-disclosure clauses.
  • Report any concerns about data protection to the ICO if you believe a company has broken the rules.
