1Password breach: Names and emails copied, vaults safe

A password manager says an attacker stole some customer names and emails, but your saved passwords and vaults were not touched.

Thursday 26 November 2026, 3.52pm

What happened

1Password has told customers that an attacker got into some of its internal systems. The company says this happened after someone accessed an employee’s work account on GitHub, a platform where developers share code. The attacker used this access to copy a limited amount of customer account information.

The stolen data includes names, email addresses, and dates accounts were created. It also includes some technical details that 1Password says cannot be used to break into your account. Importantly, the company states that your vaults, which hold your passwords, were not accessed. It says it does not have access to your passwords or secret keys.

1Password has already taken steps to fix the problem. It disabled the compromised account, removed the attacker’s access, and changed its security settings. It also made the codes used for account recovery harder to guess. The company says it found no evidence that anyone used the stolen data to take over accounts.

You do not need to change anything in your 1Password account right now. However, the company warns that scammers may use the stolen names and emails to send fake messages. These messages might look like they are from 1Password, trying to trick you into giving away your details.

Am I affected?

1Password has not said how many customers are affected or which specific accounts were targeted. If you use 1Password, you should watch for unexpected emails, texts, or calls that mention the service or ask for account recovery. Check your inbox for any messages you did not expect.

What to do

  • Watch for unexpected emails, texts, or calls that mention 1Password, invitations, or account recovery.
  • If you receive a suspicious message, do not click any links. Instead, open the official 1Password app yourself or type the website address manually.
  • Remember that 1Password will never ask you for your password or Secret Key by email or phone. If someone asks, treat it as a scam.
