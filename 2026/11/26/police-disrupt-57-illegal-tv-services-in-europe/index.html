Police disrupt 57 illegal TV services in Europe

A joint police operation has shut down 57 illegal TV streaming services and traced millions in cryptocurrency payments.

Thursday 26 November 2026, 6.11am

What happened

Police from 11 countries have disrupted 57 illegal IPTV services. IPTV is a way of watching TV channels over the internet instead of using a traditional box or antenna. Europol, the EU’s law enforcement agency, coordinated the five-day operation in Seville, Spain.

Investigators identified 321 pirate websites that receive more than 7.3 million visits a year. These services offer thousands of TV channels and live sports for a few euros a month. They do not pay the rights holders for this content. Europol says this is a criminal market worth billions of euros each year.

Officers worked with the cryptocurrency industry to trace more than €26 million in crypto. Crypto is a digital form of money that moves quickly across borders. The team followed suspicious transactions on blockchain networks, which are public digital ledgers that record these payments. This helps police find the people controlling the money.

No arrests have been announced yet. The analysis of the transactions is still ongoing. Europol says this operation marks a shift in the fight against digital piracy. By targeting the financial networks, law enforcement aims to make it harder for criminals to profit.

Am I affected?

The Audio-visual Anti-Piracy Alliance (AAPA) estimates that 60 million people in Europe use unauthorised IPTV services. If you pay for a TV service that is much cheaper than official providers, it may be illegal. Check your bank statements or app subscriptions to see if you are paying for an unauthorised service.

What to do

  • Check your bank statements and app subscriptions for any unknown TV or streaming payments.
  • Only use official, authorised TV providers to ensure you are paying for legal content.
  • If you find an unknown payment, contact your bank to report it.
