1Password breach exposes customer data, but vaults remain safe

A hacker stole some customer details from 1Password, but the company says your encrypted password vaults were not accessed.

Thursday 26 November 2026, 3.56pm

What happened

Password management company 1Password revealed on Wednesday that a hacker stole customer information and internal source code. The company launched an investigation on 19 November after finding that an employee’s corporate GitHub account may have been compromised. GitHub is a platform where developers store and share code.

The attacker used this account to deploy malicious code and steal customer records. These records include names, email addresses, and account identifiers. 1Password says the stolen data does not include your actual passwords or the contents of your vaults.

A small number of people who were setting up or recovering their accounts during the attack have been notified. The company says there is no evidence that the hacker hijacked any user accounts. It also confirmed that the keys protecting your encrypted data were not compromised.

1Password has disabled the compromised account and replaced potentially affected credentials. The investigation is still ongoing with independent experts. The company advises customers to watch out for phishing emails that might use this news to trick you.

Am I affected?

The sources do not say exactly how many users are affected. If you were in the process of setting up or recovering your account when the breach happened, you may have been notified directly. Customers do not need to take any action.

What to do

  • Be careful of phishing emails that mention this breach, as scammers may use the news to trick you.
  • No action is needed for your password vaults, as 1Password says they were not accessed.
