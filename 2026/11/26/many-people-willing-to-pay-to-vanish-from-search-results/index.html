Many people willing to pay to vanish from search results

A US survey suggests many people are willing to pay to remove their personal details from public search results, driven by fears about what artificial intelligence can infer from scattered data.

Thursday 26 November 2026, 6.08am

What happened

A survey of 1,008 US adults by privacy company Incogni found that 55% would pay to have their personal information permanently removed from public search results. The same group found that 53% would like a record of everyone who has searched for their name over the past five years.

Darius Belejevas, Head of Incogni, said the main concern is no longer just what people can find, but what artificial intelligence (AI) can guess by connecting different pieces of information. He noted that an address, old workplace, or family connection might seem harmless alone, but together they can reveal patterns about a person’s life that they never intended to make public.

The survey also highlighted how common it is for people to check their own online presence. Just over half of respondents had searched for their own name in the past five years, often before job interviews, renting a home, or going on a date. This helps them understand what a hiring manager or potential partner might discover.

However, removing information is not simple. Data brokers and people-search sites compile details like home addresses and employment histories into searchable profiles. Incogni notes that adjusting social media privacy settings will not remove this information from these third-party websites.

Am I affected?

The sources do not say who is affected. The survey was conducted in the US. You can check your own digital footprint by searching your name in a search engine to see what appears.

What to do

  • Search for your own name to see what information is currently public.
  • Review your social media privacy settings to limit who can see your posts.
  • Consider using a service to request the removal of your personal details from data broker websites.
  • Be careful about sharing personal details like your address or old workplaces online.
