Thieves are using software flaws to steal your passwords and keys

New data shows a sharp rise in criminals using known software weaknesses to steal login details and secret access keys.

Thursday 26 November 2026, 6.11am

What happened

A security company called VulnCheck has tracked how criminals exploit software flaws. Between January and November 2026, requests to steal credentials made up 13% of all recorded exploitation. This share grew from about 1% in January to between 25% and 35% in September and October.

The company tracked 694 exploited flaws during this period. Attackers used 127 of them to go after database passwords, cloud keys, and other secret codes. These secret codes, often called credentials, allow attackers to access your accounts or data without your permission.

Criminals are also targeting keys for artificial intelligence tools. VulnCheck first saw this activity in May 2026. Since then, attackers have tried to steal keys for services like OpenAI and Anthropic. They do this by looking for specific files or codes on servers that hold these secrets.

The company says this activity is now a core part of how attackers operate. They often use flaws that let them read files directly, without needing to run harmful code on the server. This makes it easier for them to find and steal sensitive information quickly.

Am I affected?

The sources do not say who is affected. You can check your account emails for unusual activity, review your bank statements for unexpected charges, and look for any security notices from the services you use.

What to do

  • Keep your software and apps up to date to fix known security flaws.
  • Use strong, unique passwords for each of your accounts.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication where possible to add an extra layer of security.
