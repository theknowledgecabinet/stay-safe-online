UK bank fraud losses rise as victims approve payments

UK bank losses from fraud where victims approve payments have risen by 22% in the first half of 2026.

Thursday 26 November 2026, 3.53pm

What happened

UK Finance, the trade body for banks, says £660m was stolen from UK bank accounts in the first six months of 2026. Of this, £316m was taken through authorised push payment (APP) fraud. This is when scammers trick you into approving a payment to them. This type of fraud is up 22% compared to the same period last year.

In contrast, unauthorised fraud, where criminals steal money without your approval, fell by 8%. UK Finance says 58% of APP fraud cases start online, such as through fake websites or emails. Another 16% start through phone or text messages. Because you authorise the payment, it bypasses the security systems banks use to block fraud.

Ruth Ray, managing director of economic crime at UK Finance, said criminals are increasingly using online platforms to target victims. She called for stronger rules for technology and telecoms companies to help stop scams at the source. Ron Warmington, a former banking investigator, added that police lack the resources to catch these fraudsters, meaning criminals rarely face consequences.

Am I affected?

The sources say this affects UK bank customers who are tricked into authorising payments. You are at risk if you receive fake messages or visit fake websites that ask you to pay money. Check your bank statements and emails for any payments you do not recognise.

What to do

  • Be wary of messages or websites that ask you to make a payment, even if they look like they are from a trusted company.
  • Contact your bank immediately if you think you have been scammed, as they may be able to help.
  • Do not click on links in unexpected emails or texts; instead, type the company's website address directly into your browser.
