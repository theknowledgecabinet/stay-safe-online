UK cyber action plan delayed until December

The government has postponed its main strategy for protecting the economy from hackers, raising concerns about national security readiness.

Friday 27 November 2026, 3.58pm

What happened

Britain has delayed the launch of its National Cyber Action Plan again. This document is the government’s strategy for defending the wider economy against state-backed and criminal hackers. It was originally due by the end of 2025 but has been pushed back multiple times.

Officials now aim to publish the plan on 9 December. A government spokesperson said ministers remain committed to releasing it, though they did not explain the latest delay. The plan is expected to focus on three areas: threat, growth, and resilience.

A House of Lords committee warned that the UK appears complacent about potentially catastrophic risks. They said the country is dangerously underprepared for crises that could disrupt everyday life. Baroness Northover, a member of the committee, said the situation is unacceptable and urged the government to act urgently.

The delay follows other setbacks in cybersecurity law. The Cyber Security and Resilience Bill, which updates critical infrastructure laws, is not expected to be enforced until 2028. Officials argue that significant work is still underway in Westminster, including new bills and funding for AI security.

Am I affected?

The sources do not say who is affected. This is a government policy delay, so there is no specific breach or scam targeting individuals right now. You do not need to check your accounts or bank statements for this specific news.

What to do

  • Keep your devices updated with the latest security patches.
  • Use strong, unique passwords for your important accounts.
  • Be careful of phishing emails that claim to be from the government or banks.
