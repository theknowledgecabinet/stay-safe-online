South Korean crypto exchange Bithumb confirms 15-day data breach

Bithumb says hackers accessed its internal system for 15 days, stealing passport numbers and bank details from customers and staff.

Friday 27 November 2026, 6.17am

What happened

South Korean cryptocurrency exchange Bithumb says hackers stole passport numbers, bank account numbers, and crypto wallet addresses. The company found that the attackers accessed an internal collaboration system called DaouOffice from 12 to 27 October. This period was much longer than the one-hour window Bithumb first reported on 29 October.

The stolen records included copies of identity documents, resident registration numbers, and transaction histories. Bithumb said the breach affected customers, employees of partner companies, and current and former staff. The specific data taken varied by person. The company did not give a new total number of affected people in its latest update.

Bithumb says the DaouOffice system is separate from its trading systems. It found no evidence that the exchange itself or the customer database was directly breached. A wallet address alone does not let someone move funds. However, real transaction details can make a fake support call sound convincing.

The company says it fixed the security flaw and is reviewing its internet-facing services. It is notifying affected customers by email and text. Bithumb is also monitoring unusual logins and withdrawals around the clock.

Am I affected?

The sources say customers, employees of partner companies, and current and former staff of Bithumb are affected. Bithumb is notifying affected customers by email and text and will tell each person which records were stolen. Customers can also open Bithumb’s website directly and use its personal data breach checker.

What to do

  • Check your email and text messages for official notifications from Bithumb.
  • If you use Bithumb, visit their website directly to use the personal data breach checker.
  • Do not share passwords or one-time codes with anyone who calls you.
  • Verify any security alerts through the official app or customer centre, not by following links in messages.
