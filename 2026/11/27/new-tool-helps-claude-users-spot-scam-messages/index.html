New tool helps Claude users spot scam messages

A new connection between Norton and the AI chatbot Claude lets users check suspicious texts and emails for fraud.

Friday 27 November 2026, 3.58pm

What happened

Norton has launched a new connector for the AI chatbot Claude. This tool brings Norton’s scam detection features directly into the chat. You do not need a separate Norton account or app to use it.

The tool has three main parts. One checks links, messages, and phone numbers for scams. Another gives advice on staying safe online. The third helps with Norton product support. You can turn each part on or off individually.

In tests, the tool classified a fake text message claiming to be from a government agency as an extortion scam. It also classified a legitimate promotional email as safe. For a suspicious cloud storage message, it flagged it as potential phishing but noted it could not be definitively confirmed as malicious without more information.

When the tool spots a scam, it explains why. It points out red flags like urgent language or fake official names. It then tells you what to do, such as not clicking links or checking the official website directly.

Am I affected?

This feature is available to people who use the Claude chatbot. You can use it to check any message or link you are unsure about. The sources do not say if it is available on all devices, but it was tested on an iPhone.

What to do

  • If you receive a suspicious message, take a screenshot and ask the tool to check it.
  • Do not click links in messages you are unsure about.
  • Visit the company’s official website directly to check your account status.
  • If a message looks like a scam, delete it and do not reply.
