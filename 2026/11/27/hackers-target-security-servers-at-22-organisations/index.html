Hackers target security servers at 22 organisations

Security researchers say hackers have taken control of security servers at 22 organisations, using known flaws to steal data and disable protections.

Friday 27 November 2026, 6.22am

What happened

A threat intelligence firm called GreyNoise says it has tracked a group of hackers targeting 22 organisations across 17 countries. The attackers compromised Check Point security management servers, which are used to control and monitor network safety.

GreyNoise says the activity looked like a cyberespionage operation, where attackers try to steal sensitive information. The group targeted government bodies, financial services, and companies in energy, transport, mining, and manufacturing. The researchers say the campaign was active between 17 October and 2 November, but they believe the attackers may still have access.

Check Point, the company that makes the servers, confirmed it is aware of the activity. A spokesperson said the attackers used a specific security flaw called CVE-2026-16232. This flaw allows an attacker to obtain an application login token and authenticate with administrator privileges. Check Point says this issue was publicly disclosed in July 2026, and a fix was available at that time.

The hackers also tried to use other known security weaknesses in different software, including systems from Citrix, Ivanti, and Microsoft. GreyNoise noted that the attackers stole firewall databases, user passwords, and internal security files. They also disabled some security policies, which effectively turned off the firewall protections for the affected organisations.

Am I affected?

The sources say the 22 victim organisations are spread across 17 countries and include government entities, financial services providers, and companies in the energy, transportation, mining, and manufacturing sectors. The sources do not list the specific names of these organisations or say if any are based in the UK. You should check if your employer or any service you use is one of these companies by looking for official notices from them.

What to do

  • Ensure your devices are updated with the latest security patches, as the attackers used known flaws that had fixes available.
  • Use strong, unique passwords for your online accounts and turn on two-factor authentication where possible.
  • Watch for unusual activity in your email or bank accounts, as the attackers stole credentials and data from the targeted organisations.
