Standard Chartered's crypto unit hit by data breach

Zodia Custody says it found no stolen crypto, but some client and employee contact details were exposed.

Friday 27 November 2026, 3.58pm

What happened

Standard Chartered’s cryptocurrency custody unit, Zodia Custody, confirmed a security breach. The company’s chief executive, Richard Clark, told Reuters that the firm detected attempts to probe its platform on 23 November 2026.

Zodia temporarily shut down its business-to-business API, a digital link that lets large clients connect their own systems to Zodia’s platform. The company said this was a precaution. It did not say the API was the attacker’s entry point.

By 24 November, Zodia found that an unauthorised party accessed publicly available details about client organisations. The exposed records also included business contact names and email addresses for a small number of clients and employees. The company contacted affected clients and notified regulators.

Zodia says its investigation has found no unauthorised transactions. The exposed information did not include private keys, which are used to authorise crypto transfers. However, a real employee name and email address can make a fake support message look credible.

Am I affected?

The sources say the breach affected a small number of clients and employees of Zodia Custody. The company did not disclose an exact count or list of names. If you work for an institution that uses Zodia, check your company’s internal notices or contact your IT team to see if you are affected.

What to do

  • If you receive an unexpected request to reconnect an API, share credentials, or approve a transfer, pause and verify it.
  • Check the request through an existing support contact rather than using a phone number or link in the message.
  • Be wary of messages that use real employee names and email addresses, as these can make scams look genuine.
