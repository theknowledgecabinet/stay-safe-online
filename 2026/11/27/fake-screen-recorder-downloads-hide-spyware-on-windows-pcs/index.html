Fake screen recorder downloads hide spyware on Windows PCs

A group called Silver Fox is using fake downloads of a popular screen recorder to install spyware that can monitor your computer.

Friday 27 November 2026, 3.58pm

What happened

A Windows user searched for the oCam screen recorder and downloaded an installer from a third-party website. The package installed the genuine app, but it also quietly launched a second program. A separate, closely related sample contained Gh0stRAT malware, which is designed to spy on your computer.

According to security firm WatchGuard, this malware can record what you type, capture your screen, and access your webcam and microphone. In this specific case, the user’s security software stopped the attack within 90 seconds. The malware did not gain a lasting foothold on the device, and no network connections were observed.

This tactic is not new. In an earlier campaign, fake Microsoft Teams downloads also installed the real application while delivering malware in the background. The goal is to make the download look safe because the expected software works. Analysts note that a high search ranking or a familiar logo does not verify where the installer came from.

Am I affected?

The sources do not say who is affected. You can check if you are affected by looking for unexpected programs or files in your computer's public documents folder. You should also check if your antivirus settings have been changed without your permission.

What to do

  • Download software only from the official publisher's website or an approved company catalogue.
  • Do not rely on search engine rankings or third-party download portals to find safe software.
  • Keep your antivirus and endpoint security software up to date to detect suspicious behaviour.
