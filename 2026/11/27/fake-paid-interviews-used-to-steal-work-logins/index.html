Fake paid interviews used to steal work logins

Criminals are sending fake invitations to paid expert interviews to trick people into giving away their work account details.

Friday 27 November 2026, 6.16am

What happened

A cybersecurity firm called Push Security has found a new scam. It uses fake emails that look like they come from big brands like Google, Nvidia, and Spotify. The emails invite people to a paid interview or webinar. They promise up to $600 for a 45-minute chat.

The emails use real services to send the links, which makes them look trustworthy. When you click the link, you see a page that looks like the company's website. It asks you to sign in with your work account to confirm your place. This is a phishing attack, which is a trick to steal your login details.

The scam targets work accounts specifically. It will not accept personal email addresses like Gmail or Yahoo. Once you type in your password, the page may ask for a security code. The criminals then use your details to log in to your account themselves.

Push Security says the scam has been active since October. It impersonates eight different companies. The firm also found a similar scam targeting Google Ads accounts using fake Coca-Cola invitations.

Am I affected?

The sources say this targets corporate accounts, not personal ones. If you use a work email, you could be affected. Check your inbox for unexpected interview offers.

What to do

  • Do not click links in unexpected emails offering money for interviews.
  • Check the sender's email address carefully for spelling mistakes.
  • If you clicked a link, change your password immediately and tell your IT team.
  • Be careful if a website asks for a security code after you log in.
