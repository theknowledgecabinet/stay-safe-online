Fake login pages built with AI found on Hugging Face

Criminals used an AI tool to create fake websites that looked like Microsoft and PayPal, tricking people into giving up their passwords.

Friday 27 November 2026, 6.17am

What happened

Security firm Aikido found 42 fake login pages hosted on the AI platform Hugging Face. These pages were built using the platform's own AI website builder, called DeepSite. The fake sites imitated 28 well-known brands, including Microsoft, PayPal and Netflix.

The pages asked users for passwords, one-time codes and payment card details. Aikido said the pages sent this stolen data to messaging apps like Telegram and Discord. Some pages also recorded the visitor's IP address before sending them to the real website.

Aikido told Hugging Face about the pages on 16 October. By 29 October, all the pages had been paused or removed. Aikido said it does not know how many people entered their details, as it did not have visitor data.

Aikido warned that a login page on a trusted platform is not proof it is real. They advised users to check their account activity if they visited any of these pages.

Am I affected?

The sources do not say who is affected. You should check your account emails for unusual sign-in alerts and review your bank statements for unrecognised charges. Also check the service's own notices for any security warnings.

What to do

  • Check your email for any sign-in alerts from services like Microsoft or PayPal.
  • If you think you entered details on a fake page, change your password immediately.
  • End all active sessions on your accounts to log out any unknown devices.
  • Review your recent sign-ins to see if there are locations or times you do not recognise.
