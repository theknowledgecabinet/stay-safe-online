Home Office reprimanded over border data errors

The UK data protection regulator has reprimanded the Home Office for failing to keep border control records accurate, which led to some people being wrongly detained.

Friday 27 November 2026, 6.20am

What happened

The Information Commissioner’s Office (ICO) has issued a formal reprimand to the Home Office. This follows a finding that the government failed to keep personal data accurate in its new border control system, called Helios.

The ICO said the Home Office introduced the system without a clear process for staff to report or fix mistakes. As a result, some people were wrongly stopped for extra checks or refused entry to the UK. The regulator noted this likely caused psychological distress for those affected.

The Home Office has since introduced a temporary system to report these errors. By March 2026, border officers had raised 6,500 incidents through this new process. Around 5,000 incorrect records have been removed as a result.

Am I affected?

The sources say this affected individuals who were wrongly stopped at the border for additional checks or refused entry. The sources do not provide a specific list of names or a way to check your own record directly.

What to do

  • Keep records of any border control issues you have experienced, such as dates and locations.
  • Check the Home Office website for any updates on how to request a review of your personal data.
  • Contact the Information Commissioner’s Office if you believe your data has been handled incorrectly.
