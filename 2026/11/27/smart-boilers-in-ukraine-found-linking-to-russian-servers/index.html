Smart boilers in Ukraine found linking to Russian servers

Researchers found that some Electrolux-branded smart boilers connect to servers in Russia, raising privacy concerns for household data.

Friday 27 November 2026, 6.20am

What happened

A user in Ukraine noticed his Electrolux smart boiler trying to connect to a server in Moscow. The research team at Mysterium VPN checked this and confirmed the device was linking to a Russian company called Rusklimat. This company operates the Hommyn app, which controls the boiler.

The boiler is made in China by a Swiss company, CladSwiss, which uses the Electrolux name under licence. The manual says the remote-control app, ClimatOn, is provided by CladSwiss, not Electrolux. However, the Russian app sends data to servers in Moscow. This includes details like when the boiler is on and the room it is in.

Mysterium VPN says Russia is not recognised by the European Commission as protecting personal data adequately. The app also sends your phone’s location to a weather service. The researchers did not connect to the servers themselves, so they cannot confirm exactly what data is sent, but the app code shows it is designed to do so.

AB Electrolux sold its Russian business in 2022 and said it would not supply the company it sold to. However, Rusklimat still sells heaters under the Electrolux name in Russia. Mysterium VPN sent questions to AB Electrolux, CladSwiss and Rozetka, but none had replied when the report was published.

Am I affected?

The sources say this issue was found in Ukraine, but the devices are sold in other countries like Serbia, Poland, and Lithuania. If you have an Electrolux smart boiler or a device from Ballu or Zanussi, check which app you use to control it. The sources don't say who is affected in the UK, so check your router logs for connections to 'rusklimat' or use DNS monitoring tools to see if your device is contacting Russian servers.

What to do

  • Check your router logs for any connections to 'rusklimat' or use DNS monitoring tools like Pi-hole or AdGuard Home.
  • If you see these connections, block the Russian server to stop remote access, though local control may still work over Wi-Fi.
  • Consider disabling the boiler's Wi-Fi if you do not need remote control.
  • Place smart appliances on a separate guest network to keep them away from your main computers and phones.
