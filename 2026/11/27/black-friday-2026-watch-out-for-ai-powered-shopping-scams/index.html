Black Friday 2026: Watch out for AI-powered shopping scams

Scammers are using AI to create fake Black Friday deals and phishing emails, so shoppers should check links carefully before paying.

Friday 27 November 2026, 3.56pm

What happened

Cybercriminals are using artificial intelligence to create convincing fake shopping websites and emails during Black Friday. These scams look almost identical to real offers from popular brands. The goal is to steal your banking details, passwords, or payment information.

Research shows a sharp rise in these attacks. Kaspersky reported that over 19 million phishing attempts targeting shoppers were blocked between January and October 2026. This is a 198.1% increase compared to the same period last year. Other firms also saw a huge spike in fake shopping websites and emails in November.

Scammers often use 'typosquatting', which means registering web addresses that look very similar to real brands but have small spelling errors. They may also use fake social media ads or malicious apps that look like discount tools. These can steal your data or install harmful software on your phone or computer.

The most dangerous part is that these scams look professional. AI helps scammers create realistic product photos and fake customer reviews. A secure connection (indicated by HTTPS) does not mean the business is trustworthy. You must check the website address carefully before entering any details.

Am I affected?

The sources say these scams target online shoppers, banking customers, and payment system users globally. You are at risk if you plan to buy items online during Black Friday or receive unexpected discount emails. Check your inbox for messages from unknown senders or links that look slightly different from the usual brand address.

What to do

  • Type the retailer's website address directly into your browser instead of clicking links in emails or ads.
  • Check for small spelling errors in web addresses and look for missing company information or refund policies.
  • Download shopping apps only from official app stores and check that the developer name is correct.
  • Be wary of discounts that seem too good to be true or requests to verify your account immediately.
