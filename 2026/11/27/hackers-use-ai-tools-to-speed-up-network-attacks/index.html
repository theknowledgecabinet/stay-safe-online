Hackers use AI tools to speed up network attacks

Researchers found that AI agents helped criminals move faster through company systems, but the speed also makes the activity easier to spot.

Friday 27 November 2026, 3.59pm

What happened

Researchers from Palo Alto Networks’ Unit 42 tracked more than 10 attacks between July and September 2026. In these cases, hackers used autonomous AI agents to steal login details and move between servers. The targets included government bodies, logistics firms, and education organisations.

The AI agents did not break in on their own. A human chose the target and gave the agent access. Once inside, the agent mapped the systems and looked for passwords. In some cases, the time between commands dropped from 23.9 seconds to just 1.4 seconds when the AI took over.

Unit 42 said the speed of these attacks actually makes them easier to detect. They noted that the AI produced bursts of commands and scripts with unusual comments. A web server suddenly running many commands is a strong sign of trouble.

The researchers found no single malware family shared across the attacks. Instead, they saw various malicious scripts, including web shells, which are scripts that let attackers run commands on compromised servers. The team recovered hundreds of these scripts but no compiled backdoor.

Am I affected?

The sources say the attacks targeted government, logistics, hospitality, manufacturing, agriculture, and education organisations. They do not specify if individual home users were directly targeted. If you work for one of these types of organisations, your employer should be checking their systems. For personal accounts, check your email for unusual login alerts and review your bank statements for any unrecognised transactions.

What to do

  • Keep your software and operating systems up to date to fix known security gaps.
  • Use strong, unique passwords for each account and turn on multi-factor authentication where possible.
  • Watch for unusual activity in your account emails, such as login alerts from new locations or devices.
  • If you work in IT, review your web server logs for sudden bursts of commands or connections to rare systems.
