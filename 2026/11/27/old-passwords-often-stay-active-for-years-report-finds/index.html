Old passwords often stay active for years, report finds

A new report shows that most old digital keys found in 2022 still worked in 2026, highlighting a slow process for removing them.

Friday 27 November 2026, 4.00pm

What happened

A report called the State of Secrets Sprawl 2026 found that more than 64% of valid secrets discovered in 2022 still worked in January 2026. The report says teams often stall on removing these secrets because they are unsure what might break if they do.

The report notes that checking only if a secret is valid misses 46% of critical secrets. It advises prioritising by impact instead. This means weighing what a credential can access and whether it leaked publicly.

A secret is a digital key, like a password or API key, that allows a program to access data. The report suggests updating how organisations handle these keys to improve security.

Am I affected?

The sources do not say who is affected. You can check your account emails for security alerts, review your bank statements for unusual activity, and look for notices from the services you use.

What to do

  • Change your passwords regularly, especially for important accounts.
  • Use a password manager to keep track of your credentials securely.
  • Check if your accounts have two-factor authentication turned on.
