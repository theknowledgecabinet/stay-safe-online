Turnitin confirms cyberattack on customer systems

The education software firm says it detected unauthorised activity and is investigating with outside experts.

Wednesday 25 November 2026, 6.15am

What happened

Turnitin has told users that it found unauthorised activity on some of its systems. The company says it has started its emergency response plan and is working with outside security specialists.

Turnitin is a well-known tool used by schools and universities to check student work for plagiarism and AI-generated text. Its CEO, Chris Caren, said in a public letter that law enforcement and cyber regulators have been notified.

The company says its systems are currently working normally and are safe to use. It also stated that the affected systems do not collect government ID details or financial information.

However, Turnitin has not said exactly what happened or if any user data was stolen. It is still monitoring its systems to make sure the threat is gone.

Am I affected?

The sources do not say who is affected. You can check your email for any official notices from Turnitin or your institution. You can also look at your bank statements to see if any unexpected charges appear.

What to do

  • Check your email for any official messages from Turnitin or your school or university.
  • Keep your passwords unique and use a password manager to store them safely.
  • Watch your bank statements for any unusual activity.
