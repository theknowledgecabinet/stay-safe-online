US government urged to fix email security bug

A flaw in a popular email filtering tool was used by hackers, prompting US officials to urge a fix before Thanksgiving.

Wednesday 25 November 2026, 6.17am

What happened

The US cybersecurity agency, CISA, has warned federal departments to patch a security flaw in N-able’s Mail Assure platform. This software helps block spam and malware from reaching inboxes. The bug, known as CVE-2026-134624, has a high severity score of 9.3.

CISA added the issue to its list of exploited vulnerabilities on Monday evening. This means hackers have already used the bug to break into one of N-able’s servers. The agency urged US government bodies to install the fix by Thanksgiving.

N-able said it fixed the bug over the weekend and restored access for its customers. The company stated that email sending and spam filtering continued to work during the incident. It also said it found no evidence of the bug being used on any other servers.

N-able promised to publish a full report on what happened within two weeks. It clarified that this incident is not linked to other security issues in its N-central software, which was discussed recently.

Am I affected?

The sources say US federal agencies were urged to patch the bug. They do not specify if UK users or other private individuals are directly affected. You can check if your email provider uses this software by looking at their security notices or contacting their support team.

What to do

  • Keep your email software and filters up to date.
  • Check your email provider’s security notices for any updates.
  • Be careful with unexpected emails, as spam filters are not perfect.
