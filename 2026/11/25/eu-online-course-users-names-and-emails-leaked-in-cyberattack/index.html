EU online course users' names and emails leaked in cyberattack

The European Commission confirmed that a cyberattack exposed the names and email addresses of people who use its online learning platform.

Wednesday 25 November 2026, 4.00pm

What happened

The European Commission has confirmed that a cyberattack on its systems led to a data breach. The attack affected the EU Academy, an online platform where people outside the Commission can take courses about EU policies. The Commission said that names and email addresses of users were exposed. It is still investigating the full scale of the leak.

The Commission’s cybersecurity team detected the attack on 22 October. They identified a tool called ARTEX, which uses artificial intelligence to find weaknesses in computer systems, as a possible tool used in the attack. The Commission has suspended access to the EU Academy while the investigation continues. It has also notified the European Data Protection Supervisor.

Spokesman Thomas Regnier said the Commission is warning users about the risk of phishing. Phishing involves fake messages that try to trick you into giving away personal details. Because the attackers now have names and emails, scam messages may look more convincing. The Commission has not contacted individuals directly but posted a general warning on the website.

Some reports suggest the attack may be linked to a group backed by China, but the Commission has not officially confirmed who is responsible. The investigation is still ongoing, and the Commission says it will only name the attackers once the inquiry is complete.

Am I affected?

The sources say that users of the EU Academy online learning platform are affected. If you have taken courses or registered for training on this specific EU platform, your name and email address may have been exposed. The sources do not say if UK residents specifically used the platform, so you should check if you have an account there.

What to do

  • Be careful with unexpected emails or messages claiming to be from the European Commission.
  • Do not share personal information or click links in messages you did not expect.
  • Visit the EU Academy website directly to check for official updates.
  • Watch for signs of phishing, such as urgent requests for passwords or money.
