Police disrupt 57 illegal streaming services

Authorities from 11 countries have disrupted 57 illegal streaming services that drew over 7.3 million visits a year.

Wednesday 25 November 2026, 4.03pm

What happened

Police from 11 countries have worked together to disrupt 57 illegal streaming services. These services offered access to TV channels, live sports, and movies without permission from the copyright owners. They charged users a small monthly fee for this unauthorised access.

Europol, the EU’s law enforcement agency, said the operation identified 321 websites involved in the scheme. Investigators traced over €26 million in cryptocurrency transactions. Cryptocurrency is a digital form of money that can be hard to track. Experts from crypto companies helped follow the money to find the people behind the services.

The European Audiovisual Anti-Piracy Alliance said legitimate providers lose around €4.83 billion a year. This is because roughly 60 million people in Europe use these illegal platforms. The joint action also involved the European Union Intellectual Property Office and INTERPOL.

This crackdown follows several other major operations in recent years. In June, a separate operation removed more than 27,000 illegal streaming links. In May, Italian authorities dismantled a piracy platform that gave illegal access to services like Netflix and Disney+.

Am I affected?

The sources say roughly 60 million people in Europe use these illegal platforms. If you have paid for a streaming service that is not an official provider, you may have been affected. Check your bank statements for small monthly payments to unknown companies.

What to do

  • Only use official streaming services that have clear terms and conditions.
  • Check your bank statements for any small, recurring payments you do not recognise.
  • If you see an unknown charge, contact your bank to dispute it and cancel the subscription.
