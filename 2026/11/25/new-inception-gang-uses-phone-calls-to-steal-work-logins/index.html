New 'Inception' gang uses phone calls to steal work logins

A new criminal group is calling employees to trick them into giving up their work passwords, but no major companies have confirmed a breach yet.

Wednesday 25 November 2026, 3.56pm

What happened

A new data extortion group called 'Inception' has launched a large-scale campaign. It targets single sign-on (SSO) credentials, which are the master keys that let you log in to many different work apps with one password.

The group says it has stolen data from seven companies, including chip maker AMD and PepsiCo. However, none of these firms have publicly confirmed a data breach. Cybersecurity firm ReversingLabs says the group has been operating since late October.

The attacks rely on phone calls rather than emails. Callers pose as support staff and pressure employees to reset their passwords through fake sign-in pages. These pages look like official legal or security sites to trick users into typing in their details.

Security company Okta links this group to a previous gang called ShinyHunters. Okta says the group likely changed its name after members were arrested. The Inception group denies this link, claiming it operates independently.

Am I affected?

The sources list AMD, PepsiCo, Cigna Healthcare, AutoZone, and Lucid Motors as claimed victims, but note that none have publicly confirmed a breach. You can check your account emails, your bank statements, and the service's own notices for any signs of unusual activity.

What to do

  • Be wary of phone calls from 'support' asking you to reset your password or verify your identity.
  • Use passkeys or FIDO2 security keys for your work accounts if your employer offers them, as these are harder to steal.
  • If you work in IT, check your network logs for signs of suspicious activity dating back to 21 October 2026.
