AI tool used to build fake login pages for Microsoft and Google

Criminals used an AI website builder to create fake sign-in pages that looked like major brands, putting passwords at risk.

Wednesday 25 November 2026, 3.56pm

What happened

Security researchers at Aikido found 42 fake web pages on the Hugging Face platform. These pages copied the look of sign-in screens for 28 brands, including Microsoft, Google, PayPal, and Coinbase. The researchers say the pages were built using an AI tool called DeepSite.

The pages were found in October 2026. At the time, 34 were live and eight were paused. By the end of the month, all 42 pages were paused or no longer reachable. The researchers could not say how many people visited the pages or entered their details.

Some fake pages asked for one-time codes, card details, or personal information. One fake Microsoft page even sent visitors to the real Outlook site after collecting their data. This makes it hard for people to tell if they are on a safe page.

The risk came from the content hosted on the platform, not from visiting Hugging Face itself. The secure connection (HTTPS) on these pages did not prove that the brands owned them.

Am I affected?

The sources do not say who is affected. You can check if you are affected by looking for emails with links to 'hf.space' addresses. If you clicked such a link and entered your password, you should assume your account is at risk.

What to do

  • If you received an email asking you to sign in, do not click the link. Instead, type the website address yourself or use a saved bookmark.
  • If you entered your password on a suspicious page, change it immediately and check your account for any unfamiliar sign-ins.
  • If you entered bank or card details, contact your bank right away.
  • Look for unexpected links in your emails that end in 'hf.space' and report them.
