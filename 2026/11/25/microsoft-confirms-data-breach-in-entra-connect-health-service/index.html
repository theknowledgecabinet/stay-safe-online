Microsoft confirms data breach in Entra Connect Health service

Microsoft has fixed a flaw that let attackers view some business details, but says no passwords were stolen.

Wednesday 25 November 2026, 4.07pm

What happened

Microsoft has disclosed a data breach in its Entra Connect Health service. This is a cloud tool that helps company IT teams check if their identity systems are working correctly. The company said it found a weakness in the service on 9 November 2026. This flaw had been present since 23 July 2026.

Microsoft fixed the problem on 19 November 2026. It says the issue was fixed on its servers, so customers do not need to install any software updates. The company confirmed that no passwords, security tokens, or keys were stolen. However, attackers could view some business information, such as administrator email addresses and domain names.

Microsoft says this information could be used to send convincing fake messages, known as phishing. These messages might try to trick staff into giving away more details. The company has not said how many businesses were affected. It advises organisations to check their own records for any unusual activity.

Am I affected?

The sources do not say who is affected. Microsoft has not disclosed how many tenants were affected. You should check if your organisation received a specific notification from Microsoft. If you work for a business, ask your IT team if they have reviewed their security logs.

What to do

  • If you work for a business, tell your IT team to watch for suspicious emails that look like they are from Microsoft or your IT staff.
  • Make sure your work accounts use strong two-factor authentication that is hard to fake.
  • Check official Microsoft service health pages directly, rather than clicking links in emails, to verify any security notices.
