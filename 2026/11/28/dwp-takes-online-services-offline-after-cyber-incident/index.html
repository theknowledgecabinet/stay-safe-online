DWP takes online services offline after cyber incident

The Department for Work and Pensions has shut down some online services as a precaution, but regular benefit and pension payments are continuing.

Saturday 28 November 2026, 3.45pm

What happened

The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) said on Friday that it had experienced a cyber incident. As a result, some online services were taken offline as a precaution. The department has not explained exactly what caused the incident or confirmed if hackers were involved.

The DWP is the largest government department by spending and manages the state pension and other benefits. It makes around 2.5 million payments every day. A spokesperson said regular benefit and pension payments are not affected and will be paid as normal. The department is working with the National Cyber Security Centre to investigate and bring services back online quickly.

Some people who rely on these services have reported delays in receiving money or accessing their accounts. The DWP said it does not believe these delays are linked to the cyber incident. It also said it did not believe any personal information had been lost or stolen. The government is working to resolve the issue to minimise disruption.

Am I affected?

The DWP says regular benefit and pension payments are not affected and will be paid as normal. If you use DWP online services, you may find them temporarily unavailable. The sources do not say who is affected regarding data loss, but the department stated it does not believe personal information was stolen.

What to do

  • Check your bank account to see if your regular payments have arrived as normal.
  • If you are experiencing delays or issues with payments, call the DWP helplines in the usual way.
  • Do not panic if online services are down; the department says this is a precaution.
  • Customers directly affected by the incident will not face sanctions for missing deadlines while services are offline.
