Guadeloupe hospital hit by suspected ransomware attack

A suspected ransomware attack has forced a major French hospital to pause some surgeries and cancer treatments, reverting to pen and paper.

Saturday 28 November 2026, 6.22am

What happened

France’s national cybersecurity agency, ANSSI, is investigating a suspected ransomware attack on the University Hospital of Guadeloupe. Ransomware is a type of malicious software that locks or encrypts files, making them unusable until a demand is met. The agency said the incident appeared to involve this type of attack and is checking if hackers gained access through an IT supplier that was reportedly compromised earlier.

The hospital, which serves the French Caribbean island of Guadeloupe, has had to cancel some surgical procedures, medical consultations, and imaging tests. Radiation therapy has been temporarily suspended for safety reasons, and chemotherapy sessions scheduled for Monday were cancelled. However, emergency services, dialysis treatments, and urgent cardiac procedures remain available.

Hospital staff first noticed computer and telephone outages on Saturday evening. What looked like a technical fault turned out to be a cyberattack that encrypted the hospital’s databases. As of Tuesday, healthcare workers could not access electronic medical records and had to process prescriptions manually. The hospital director told local media: “We’re back to pen and paper, relying on couriers who travel from one department to another.”

It is not yet clear who is behind the attack or if any personal or medical information was stolen. The hospital has not received a ransom demand and has ruled out paying one. French prosecutors have launched a criminal investigation. The hospital plans to restore systems gradually after checking them for malware, a process expected to take several days.

Am I affected?

The sources say the attack affected the University Hospital of Guadeloupe in France. The hospital said it would notify anyone whose data was compromised.

What to do

  • Watch out for phishing - fake messages that try to trick you into giving away details - from criminals posing as hospital staff.
  • Bring paper copies of your medical records, prescriptions, and previous test results to appointments if you are a patient at this hospital.
  • Only visit the hospital’s emergency departments if you need immediate medical care.
