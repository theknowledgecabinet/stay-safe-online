pCloud confirms password manager hack after suspicious login alerts

pCloud has confirmed a cyberattack on its password manager.

Saturday 28 November 2026, 6.17am

What happened

pCloud has confirmed that its password manager, pCloud Pass, suffered a cyberattack. The company says the incident was quickly contained. Users began reporting that they received email alerts saying a new device had connected to their accounts. Some of these alerts appeared even though the users had two-factor authentication enabled.

Two-factor authentication is a security step that asks for a code or confirmation on your phone to prove it is really you. pCloud says the attack was isolated to the password manager and did not affect its cloud storage services. The company states that passwords and notes stored in the app have not been compromised because they are encrypted on your device. This means they are scrambled and can only be read using your master password.

However, pCloud did not say whether any encrypted password vaults were accessed or stolen. If attackers get these encrypted files, they could try to crack them offline without being subject to the service's login rate limits or two-factor authentication protections. pCloud is also warning customers to watch out for phishing, which are fake messages that try to trick you into giving away details.

This incident follows other attacks on password managers this month. Earlier in November, mSecure warned customers to change stored passwords after a hacker reportedly stole an encrypted database. On Wednesday, 1Password disclosed a data breach after an employee's account was compromised. 1Password said its customers' password vaults were not compromised.

Am I affected?

The sources say a small number of pCloud Pass user email addresses were exposed. You can tell if you are affected by checking your email for notifications from pCloud about a new device connecting to your account. If you do not use pCloud Pass, the sources say the attack was isolated to that service and did not affect cloud storage.

What to do

  • Check your email for alerts from pCloud about new devices connecting to your account.
  • Review the devices connected to your pCloud Pass account to see if any are unfamiliar.
  • Be careful of phishing emails; pCloud says it will never ask for your master password or recovery words over email, phone, or chat.
  • If you use other password managers, check their official websites for any security notices.
