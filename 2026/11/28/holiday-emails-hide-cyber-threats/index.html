Holiday emails hide cyber threats

Researchers investigated five scams using Thanksgiving and Black Friday themes to steal passwords and install harmful software.

Saturday 28 November 2026, 3.49pm

What happened

Security researchers at ANY.RUN have investigated five cyberattacks that use holiday themes to trick people. These attacks target both businesses and users during the Thanksgiving and Black Friday period.

The scams include fake event invitations, greeting cards, and discount offers. Attackers use these familiar messages to hide dangerous links and files. For example, one fake invitation led to a page that stole corporate login details.

Another attack used a fake e-card to automatically download remote access software. This software, called ScreenConnect, is legitimate but dangerous if installed without permission. It can give attackers control over your computer.

Other scams involved fake surveys and discount coupons. One fake survey asked for personal data and a small payment fee. Another fake coupon file contained a hidden script that tried to install further malware on the device.

Am I affected?

The sources say these attacks target US businesses and users, including those using healthcare portals and online shopping sites. You are affected if you receive unexpected holiday-themed emails or attachments. Check your inbox for messages asking for login details or offering unusual discounts.

What to do

  • Be careful with emails that ask you to click links or open attachments, even if they look like holiday greetings.
  • Do not enter your password on pages you did not type directly into your browser.
  • Check the sender's email address carefully to see if it looks suspicious or misspelled.
  • If you click a link by mistake, change your password immediately and contact your bank or IT team.
