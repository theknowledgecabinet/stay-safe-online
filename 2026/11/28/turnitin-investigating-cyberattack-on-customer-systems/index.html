Turnitin investigating cyberattack on customer systems

The plagiarism checking service Turnitin has disclosed a cyberattack involving 'unauthorized activity', though it says its systems are safe to use and no government or financial data was collected.

Saturday 28 November 2026, 6.22am

What happened

Turnitin, a platform used by thousands of education and research institutions for plagiarism checking, has disclosed a cyberattack involving certain customer-facing systems. The company is a subsidiary of Advance Publications and provides tools for students and instructors.

CEO Chris Caren said in a letter on 23 November that the company identified unauthorised activity involving certain customer-facing systems. These systems include the submission portal for students, grading interfaces for instructors, and reporting dashboards. Turnitin did not say if any or which of these systems was breached.

The company immediately started its incident response plan and notified the relevant authorities. It also launched an investigation with help from external cyber experts. Caren stated that the systems are fully operational and safe to use.

Turnitin confirmed that these customer-facing systems do not collect government identification credentials or institutional financial information. The company is still monitoring the affected customer-facing systems while the investigation continues to determine the nature and scope of the incident.

Am I affected?

The sources do not say who is affected. Turnitin has not said if any data was accessed or how many customers might have been affected. You should check your account emails and the service's own notices for any specific updates from your institution.

What to do

  • Check your email for any official messages from Turnitin or your university or college.
  • Keep your passwords up to date and use a unique password for your Turnitin account.
  • Monitor your account for any unusual activity or login alerts.
