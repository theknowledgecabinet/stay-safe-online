US launches major crackdown on global scam networks

US prosecutors have charged over 800 people in a major crackdown on scam operations that target Americans.

Saturday 28 November 2026, 3.49pm

What happened

The US Department of Justice (DOJ) announced on 24 November that it has pursued more than 400 scam cases since March. These cases involve over 800 defendants across 85 federal districts. The effort follows an executive order signed by President Donald Trump in March, which directed federal agencies to prioritise cybercrime and fraud.

The FBI said it helped disrupt or dismantle more than 29 scam compounds in the Middle East, Africa and Asia under Operation Blackout. The agency stated it restrained nearly $17bn in criminal funds. It also reported helping free and return more than 2,200 people who were trafficked into these compounds.

US prosecutors supported law enforcement in more than 10 countries during raids on compounds and call centres. The DOJ said 30 defendants had been extradited to the US. Assistant Attorney General A. Tysen Duva said the department would not let scammers "hide behind their screens or escape justice by committing these crimes abroad."

Am I affected?

The sources say these scams targeted Americans. The DOJ cited research estimating that scams cost Americans $68bn in 2025, with about one in 10 US adults affected either directly or via a household member.

What to do

  • Be wary of unsolicited calls or messages asking for money or personal details.
  • Check your bank statements regularly for any unrecognised transactions.
  • Verify the identity of anyone asking for financial help by calling them back on a known number.
