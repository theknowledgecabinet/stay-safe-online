ShinyHunters claims return as new group Inception denies links

A message on a cybercrime forum claims the notorious ShinyHunters group is back, while a new group called Inception denies any connection.

Saturday 28 November 2026, 6.20am

What happened

Someone claiming to represent ShinyHunters has announced the group's return. This message was posted on a cybercrime forum by an account linked to the group since 2020. The post included the address of a new leak site and a new digital key to verify future messages. The authors said they would not be active on other forums and denied ties to several other hackers claiming to be members.

However, experts note that the original digital key used to prove identity had reportedly been leaked. This means the new message could be from impostors trying to use the group's reputation. The announcement follows the arrest of several individuals suspected of being involved in ShinyHunters attacks, including a Canada-based expert and a Jordanian national believed to be the group leader.

Separately, a new cybercrime group called Inception has emerged. Its leak site lists several major US companies, including AMD, PepsiCo, and Cigna Healthcare. The group says it has leaked hundreds of gigabytes of data, but the authenticity of this data has not been independently verified. Okta, a security company, says Inception may involve hackers previously associated with ShinyHunters.

Inception has denied these links in a statement on its website. The group said the evidence amounts to shared use of common services and overlapping targets, which does not prove they are the same people. They stated: “We are not ShinyHunters. We were never ShinyHunters. We are Inception.”

Am I affected?

The sources do not say who is affected. You can check your account emails for unexpected login alerts, review your bank statements for unrecognised charges, and look for official notices from services you use.

What to do

  • Be wary of messages claiming to be from known hacking groups, as impostors may use their names.
  • Keep your passwords unique for each account to limit damage if one is compromised.
  • Enable two-factor authentication where possible to add an extra layer of security.
