Nearly half of young men in the UK have lost a combined total of £11bn to social media investment scams

A survey suggests nearly half of young men in the UK have lost money to fake investment tips on social media.

Saturday 28 November 2026, 3.48pm

What happened

A survey by Starling Bank found that 44% of young men in the UK have fallen for investment scams. These scams are often pitched through social media accounts, influencers, and artificial intelligence (AI) tools. The group calls this network the “Moneysphere”. It involves anonymous accounts selling get-rich-quick dreams and hiding the risks of investments.

The survey covered over 2,000 men aged 14 to 29. It estimates that over two million young men have been victims. The average loss was just over £5,000, bringing the total to nearly £11bn. In contrast, 34% of young women in the same age group reported falling for these scams.

Starling Bank says social media algorithms push users into echo chambers of unregulated financial advice. Bernadette Smith, a senior officer at Starling, said the “Moneysphere” is a powerful force threatening young men. She urged people to question anything that feels too good to be true.

Rapper Professor Green, who spoke with victims, said many young people are being sold a lie. He noted that social media is highly personalised, meaning if you watch one scam, you will see many more. This makes it dangerous for young people trying to improve their finances without expert guidance.

Am I affected?

The sources say young men aged 14 to 29 in the UK are the main target, with 44% having fallen for these scams. Young women in the same age group are also affected, with 34% reporting losses.

What to do

  • Take a step back when presented with any investment opportunity.
  • Look out for red flags and question anything that feels too good to be true.
  • Be aware that social media algorithms may show you more scam content after you watch one.
