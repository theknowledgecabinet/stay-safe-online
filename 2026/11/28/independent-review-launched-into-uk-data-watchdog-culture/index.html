Independent review launched into UK data watchdog culture

An independent review has been appointed to investigate claims of a toxic workplace culture at the Information Commissioner's Office.

Saturday 28 November 2026, 6.20am

What happened

Barrister Karon Monaghan KC has been appointed to lead an independent review of the UK’s data protection regulator, the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO). This follows an internal investigation into the former chief, John Edwards, who resigned after being accused of using vulgar and highly sexualised language towards women.

The review will look at the culture, accountability, and governance of the organisation. It will examine how complaints are raised and handled, but it does not have the power to discipline staff or determine misconduct. The Information Commission has confirmed that non-disclosure agreements will not be enforced, allowing current and former staff to speak freely to the review team.

Staff surveys and union representatives have raised concerns about bullying and harassment within the organisation. A recent survey found that a high proportion of staff witnessed such behaviour, and many did not feel safe to speak up. Unions campaigned for this independent review after the ICO initially proposed a smaller, internal investigation.

The review is expected to report back in the first quarter of next year. The Secretary of State for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport, Lisa Nandy, said she wants staff to feel able to speak openly about their experiences and know they will be taken seriously.

Am I affected?

The sources do not say who is affected. This story concerns the internal workplace culture of the UK's data regulator, not a data breach or security threat to individual users.

What to do

  • No action is required for the general public regarding this workplace review.
  • Continue to use the ICO's online tools to check if your personal data has been involved in any separate data breaches.
  • If you work in the public sector, review your organisation's policies on speaking up about workplace concerns.
