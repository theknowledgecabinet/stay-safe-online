FBI investigating apparent hack of national crime database

The US Federal Bureau of Investigation is looking into a suspected breach of its main criminal justice database, though the full scale of the issue is not yet clear.

Monday 23 November 2026, 9.00pm

What happened

The FBI is investigating an apparent hack of the National Crime Information Center, or NCIC. This is a computerised database that holds criminal justice records, such as details on fugitives, missing persons, and stolen property. It is used by local, state, and federal law enforcement agencies.

A person familiar with the matter told the Associated Press that the FBI is looking into the incident. The FBI declined to comment on the apparent hack. The scope of the problem was not immediately clear.

President Donald Trump said in an interview that he had just heard about it and that it was "not good". This news comes as the US has seen a series of digital outages in government and private sectors. Officials have said those outages were caused by routine system upgrades, not cyberattacks. There was no immediate indication that the FBI incident was related to those outages.

Am I affected?

The sources do not say who is affected. The database is used by law enforcement agencies, not the general public. You should check your email for official notices from services you use and review your bank statements for any unusual activity.

What to do

  • Keep your passwords unique and use a password manager to store them safely.
  • Be careful of phishing emails - fake messages that try to trick you into giving away details - especially if they claim to be from official bodies.
  • Check your bank statements regularly for any unrecognised transactions.
