Cisco video system flaw being used in attacks

US security officials say criminals are using a serious flaw in Cisco video calling equipment to break into networks.

Monday 23 November 2026, 8.59pm

What happened

The US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) has warned that attackers are actively using a security flaw in Cisco Expressway devices. These are specialised machines that help companies connect their internal video calling systems to the internet securely.

Cisco released a fix for this issue in late October. However, CISA has now added the flaw to its list of vulnerabilities being used in real-world attacks. The agency told US government departments to install the update within three days.

A Russian security company called F6 said it saw attackers use this flaw to gain full control of devices connected to the internet. Once inside, the attackers tried to move around the network to steal sensitive information. CISA has urged all organisations, including private companies, to patch their systems quickly to stop these attacks.

Am I affected?

The sources say this affects organisations that use Cisco Expressway devices to connect their internal video systems to the internet. If you work for a company that uses Cisco video calling equipment, you should check with your IT team to see if they have installed the latest security update.

What to do

  • Ask your employer's IT department if they use Cisco Expressway devices.
  • Check if your organisation has installed the security patch released by Cisco in late October.
  • Keep your own personal devices updated with the latest software to stay safe from other common threats.
