Bromcom breach exposes school staff and pupil emails

A UK education software provider confirmed that email addresses for 355k people were exposed in a September 2026 data breach.

Monday 23 November 2026, 9.00pm

What happened

In September 2026, Bromcom, a UK education software provider, suffered a data breach. The incident involved a legacy single sign-on registration system. Single sign-on is a way to use one set of login details for multiple services.

Bromcom confirmed that 355k unique email addresses were exposed. These belonged to staff and pupils at UK schools. The company also said that registration reference numbers were leaked.

However, Bromcom stated that no passwords or security tokens were exposed. The company confirmed the incident did not allow access to Microsoft or Google accounts. It also did not enable access to its school management system.

The breach was added to the Have I Been Pwned database on 23 November 2026. This site helps people check if their details have been leaked in past incidents.

Am I affected?

The sources say that staff and pupils at UK schools are affected. You can check if your email address is on the list by visiting the Have I Been Pwned website. If you are unsure, you can also check your email inbox for any official notices from Bromcom or your school.

What to do

  • Change your password immediately on any account where you used the same password as your Bromcom login.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication for your accounts if the service offers it.
  • Use a password manager to create and store strong, unique passwords for each of your accounts.
