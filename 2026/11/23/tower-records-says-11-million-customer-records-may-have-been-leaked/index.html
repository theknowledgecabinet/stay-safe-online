Tower Records says 11 million customer records may have been leaked

A Japanese music retailer says a cyberattack may have exposed personal details for millions of customers, though it claims no credit card data was taken.

Monday 23 November 2026, 8.58pm

What happened

Tower Records, a Japanese music retailer, has announced that a cyberattack on 19 November 2026 may have compromised approximately 11 million customer records. The company stated that the leaked data includes personal details such as names, dates of birth, and delivery addresses. However, Tower Records said that no credit card information was included in the breach.

The company made this announcement following reports in Japan about the incident. It is important to note that these figures are based on the company's own assessment of the damage. There is no further information yet on how the attackers gained access to the system or what they did with the data.

If you have an account with Tower Records, you should check your email for any official notices from the company. They may provide specific advice on how to protect your account or what steps to take next. Keep an eye on your bank statements for any unusual activity, although the company says card details were not leaked.

Am I affected?

The sources say that approximately 11 million customer records of Tower Records may have been compromised. The sources don't say who is affected. You can check if you are affected by looking for emails from the service, checking your bank statements for unusual activity, or looking for notices on the service's website.

What to do

  • Check your email for any official messages from Tower Records regarding the breach.
  • Monitor your bank statements and credit reports for any signs of unusual activity.
  • Change your password for your Tower Records account if you use the same password on other sites.
  • Be wary of phishing emails asking for your personal details, as scammers may use this breach to target you.
