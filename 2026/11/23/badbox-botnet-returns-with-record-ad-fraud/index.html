BADBOX botnet returns with record ad fraud

A large network of infected Android devices is sending billions of fake ad requests daily, according to security researchers.

Monday 23 November 2026, 9.00pm

What happened

Security firm HUMAN says the BADBOX botnet has returned after being disrupted last year. It is now generating a record 40 billion fraudulent advertising bid requests every day. These requests come from infected Android-based devices, such as phones, tablets, and streaming TV boxes.

The malware plays video ads in hidden windows to trick advertisers into paying for views that never happened. It also uses your device to hide other illegal traffic, making it look like it is coming from a normal home connection. HUMAN says this new version is more than three times larger than the previous record holder.

Researchers linked the malware to a specific Chinese electronics manufacturer. The infected devices fetch malware modules from public code repositories, while the same payloads are also mirrored across various hosting platforms for redundancy and to resist takedowns.

HUMAN and Google have taken down some of the control servers and removed apps that spread the malware. However, HUMAN warns the operators will likely adapt and try to rebuild the network again.

Am I affected?

The sources say the malware infects cheap phones, tablets, and streaming TV boxes running the Android Open Source Project. The sources don't say who is affected.

What to do

  • Only buy certified Android devices from reputable manufacturers.
  • Ensure Play Protect is available and active on your Android devices.
