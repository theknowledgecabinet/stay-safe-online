Norway says hackers used Microsoft flaw to target energy firms

Norwegian authorities say hackers exploited a security weakness in Microsoft's cloud service to try to steal sensitive details from energy companies.

Monday 23 November 2026, 8.54pm

What happened

Norway's National Security Authority (NSM) says an unidentified group of hackers has been using a security flaw in Microsoft's cloud service to target sensitive information. The service, called Azure API Management, helps organisations manage connections between their different computer systems.

Two major Norwegian energy companies, Equinor and Skagerak Energi, reported cyberattacks last week. Equinor said it detected the attack on Tuesday but did not fully stop it until Friday. The company added that there is no sign that data was stolen or changed, and that its oil and gas production continued normally.

Microsoft told Recorded Future News that it found the issue on 18 November and fixed it the next day. The company warned that hackers may have accessed passwords and other sensitive information stored in the vulnerable systems before the fix was applied. Microsoft said the problem mainly affected customers who had not followed its security advice or who stored sensitive data directly in the service instead of using a separate, more secure tool called Azure Key Vault.

Norwegian officials say the vulnerability affected organisations in several sectors, both in Norway and abroad. Martin Albert-Hoff, the director of operational cybersecurity at NSM, said it remains unclear how widely the flaw has been used. He warned that more hackers may try to exploit it now that it is publicly known.

What to do

  • Check your email for any security alerts from Microsoft or other cloud service providers.
  • Review your bank statements and online accounts for any signs of unauthorised access.
  • Keep your software up to date to ensure you have the latest security fixes.
