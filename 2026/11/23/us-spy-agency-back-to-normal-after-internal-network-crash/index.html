US spy agency back to normal after internal network crash

The US National Security Agency has returned to normal operations after a software update caused its internal network to fail for several days.

Monday 23 November 2026, 9.03pm

What happened

The National Security Agency (NSA) says it is now operating under normal conditions. This follows a week when its internal communications network, called NSANet, crashed for much of the time.

The disruptions were caused by maintenance work on PHP, a common programming language used to build websites and apps. An NSA spokesperson confirmed the agency is back to business as usual, though some facilities are still running with limited staff.

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton said the patches caused some systems to fail to restart as expected. He added that the problems were not caused by a cyberattack. Officials also said the issues were not connected to recent IT problems affecting Microsoft products or airline services.

Am I affected?

The sources do not say who is affected. This incident happened inside a US government agency's internal network. You should check your account emails and bank statements for any unusual activity, and look for official notices from the services you use.

What to do

  • Keep your devices and apps updated with the latest security fixes.
  • Be careful with unexpected messages asking for your login details, as these could be phishing - fake messages that try to trick you.
  • Check your bank statements regularly for any transactions you do not recognise.
