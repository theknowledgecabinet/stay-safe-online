AI tool used to find flaw in medical imaging software

Amazon says Chinese hackers used an AI tool to find a security gap in a medical imaging app, showing how fast automated attacks can now happen.

Monday 23 November 2026, 8.36pm

What happened

Amazon’s security team found a server left open to the public. It showed the history of an AI tool called Cairn being used by attackers. The tool is designed to help test computer systems for weaknesses.

The attackers told the AI to “obtain shell access” to university servers in East Asia. The AI tried many ways to break in, including guessing passwords. Most attempts failed, but one succeeded.

The AI found a flaw in an open-source medical imaging application called MONAI Label. This flaw allowed the AI to control the server. Amazon says the flaw was a “zero-day”, meaning it was unknown to the software makers before this attack.

The software makers fixed the bug on 18 November 2026. Amazon worked with a government partner to warn the affected universities. The attackers did not name the specific universities or countries involved.

Am I affected?

The sources say the attacks targeted universities in East Asia. They do not say if UK organisations or individuals were affected. If you use medical imaging software, check for updates from the maker.

What to do

  • Keep all software up to date to fix known security gaps.
  • Do not leave servers or tools open to the public internet without security checks.
  • Check your organisation’s security notices for any specific warnings.
