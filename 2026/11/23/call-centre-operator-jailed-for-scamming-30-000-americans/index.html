Call centre operator jailed for scamming 30,000 Americans

A Peruvian call centre operator was sentenced to eight years in prison for helping steal millions of dollars from over 30,000 Spanish-speaking US residents in a years-long government-impersonation fraud scheme.

Monday 23 November 2026, 8.59pm

What happened

Carla Magaly Alcedo Mendoza, a 45-year-old from Lima, Peru, pleaded guilty to conspiracy to commit mail and wire fraud. She was sentenced to eight years in prison for her role in a large-scale fraud scheme. The Department of Justice said the scheme defrauded more than 30,000 Spanish-speaking residents of the United States out of $15 million.

The scammers first contacted victims claiming they qualified for free services or products. They then impersonated US government officials and lawyers. They told victims they were contractually obligated to pay large sums to receive the services.

According to court documents, Alcedo managed and operated Peruvian call centres between January 2013 and December 2018. She and her accomplices threatened victims with court action, arrest, and immigration consequences. The US Attorney said the criminals used fear and intimidation to steal millions of dollars.

This case follows a June 2026 warning from the US Federal Trade Commission. It stated that Americans lost $3.5 billion to impersonation scams in 2025. Approximately $920 million of that was stolen by government impersonators.

Am I affected?

The sources say this scheme targeted Spanish-speaking residents of the United States. The sources don't say who is affected in the UK. You can check your bank statements for any unrecognised payments and review emails for suspicious requests for money.

What to do

  • Ignore unsolicited calls claiming you owe money to a government agency.
  • Hang up if a caller threatens arrest or legal action to pressure you into paying.
  • Check your bank statements regularly for any unauthorised transactions.
