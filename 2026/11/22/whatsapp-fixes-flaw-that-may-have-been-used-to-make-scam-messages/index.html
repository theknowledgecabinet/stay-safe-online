WhatsApp fixes flaw that may have been used to make scam messages look real

WhatsApp has fixed a security gap that scammers may have used to make their messages appear more trustworthy on Android phones.

Sunday 22 November 2026, 6.44pm

What happened

WhatsApp has patched a low-impact flaw in its Android apps. The company says it found the issue while disrupting a scam campaign. A WhatsApp spokesperson told SecurityWeek that they believe a scammer was using it to make their scam message seem more credible.

The flaw, tracked as CVE-2026-23871, is linked to 'Click to WhatsApp' ads. It affects both WhatsApp and WhatsApp Business for Android. If a specific type of ad message was displayed, it could have revealed your IP address (your device's unique internet location) to an attacker.

WhatsApp also fixed two other medium-impact issues. One bug could have allowed an attacker to delete WhatsApp files if you tapped a malicious 'order details' message from an unofficial app. The other involved a security check for Meta AI. WhatsApp says there is no evidence that either of these was exploited.

The company has released updates to fix all three problems.

Am I affected?

This affects people using WhatsApp or WhatsApp Business on Android phones. You are affected if you were using a version older than 2.26.17.4. The sources do not say if you can check your specific version number easily, but updating the app fixes it.

What to do

  • Update WhatsApp to the latest version on your Android phone.
  • Check that WhatsApp Business is also updated if you use it.
  • Be careful with messages that look like ads or orders from unknown numbers.
