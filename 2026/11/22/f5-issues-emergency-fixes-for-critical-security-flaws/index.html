F5 issues emergency fixes for critical security flaws

A major US tech firm has released urgent updates to fix serious weaknesses in its network equipment, which could allow criminals to take control of systems.

Sunday 22 November 2026, 6.47pm

What happened

US cybersecurity company F5 has warned customers to install emergency patches for five critical security flaws in its BIG-IP products. These weaknesses, known as remote code execution (RCE) flaws, allow attackers to run harmful commands on the device without needing a login. F5 said these issues are rated as critical severity.

The company also released a fix for a high-severity flaw that could cause the system to crash, a problem known as a denial-of-service (DoS) attack. F5 stated it has no other way to protect these systems besides installing the new updates. It advised security teams to apply the hotfixes released on Wednesday to vulnerable devices.

While F5 has not confirmed that these specific flaws are being used in attacks right now, it noted that cybercriminals and state-backed groups have often abused similar F5 vulnerabilities in the past. The US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) has previously flagged other F5 flaws as actively exploited. Therefore, F5 recommends upgrading systems to the latest version as soon as possible.

Am I affected?

This story affects organisations that use F5 BIG-IP hardware to manage their network traffic. If you are an individual at home, you are unlikely to be directly affected unless your employer or a service you use relies on this specific equipment. The sources do not provide a way for ordinary people to check if their home internet connection uses this device.

What to do

  • If you manage IT systems, check if you use F5 BIG-IP products and install the emergency patches released on Wednesday.
  • If you are not an IT professional, ensure your personal devices are up to date with standard security updates.
  • Stay alert for unusual behaviour on work or business systems and report it to your IT team immediately.
