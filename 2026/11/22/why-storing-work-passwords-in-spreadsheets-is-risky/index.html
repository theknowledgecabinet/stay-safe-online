Why storing work passwords in spreadsheets is risky

Spreadsheets often miss new or changed passwords, leaving companies unaware of weak or shared logins that attackers can exploit.

Sunday 22 November 2026, 6.46pm

What happened

The password spreadsheet your admins use may look like a complete inventory, but it only reflects some of the credentials employees have reported using. More specifically, that spreadsheet doesn’t show passwords that have been changed, created, or shared since the document was last updated, until someone brings it up to date. That’s assuming someone ever does.

People often use easier methods to save passwords when official processes are slow. They might save logins in their web browser, send them in chat messages, or write them on sticky notes. A report by NordPass found that "123456" is the most common password used in a corporate setting. This shows that many people take the easy route when choosing passwords.

If a spreadsheet is out of date, it creates blind spots. Attackers who break into a company network often search for files with names like "password" or "login". If they find a spreadsheet, they can use the details inside to access other accounts. This can help them spread the attack further across the company.

To fix this, organisations need a better way to manage credentials. A business password manager provides a secure, central place to store work logins. It allows managers to see who has access to which accounts and check if passwords are weak or reused. This helps them spot problems early and keep company data safer.

Am I affected?

If you save work logins in your browser, send them in chat, or write them down, your company may not know about them.

What to do

  • Check if your company uses a spreadsheet to track passwords and ask your IT team about their security plan.
  • Use a password manager to store work logins instead of saving them in your browser or writing them down.
  • Avoid reusing the same password for different work accounts to limit the damage if one is compromised.
  • Report any shared or changed passwords to your IT team so they can update their records.
