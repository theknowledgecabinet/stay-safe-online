Ofcom lists four ways to check if you are 16 for social media ban

Ofcom has published a report identifying four reliable methods social media firms can use to verify users are over 16, ahead of the UK's upcoming ban.

Sunday 22 November 2026, 6.46pm

What happened

Ofcom has published a rapid assessment on how social media companies can reliably determine if a user is aged 16 or over. This comes ahead of the UK Government setting out the practical rules for a social media ban for under-16s, which is expected to come into force next year.

The regulator identified four methods that are capable of being 'highly effective' at this age. These are photo ID matching, digital ID services, banking-based checks, and facial age estimation. Photo ID matching compares a document like a passport or driving licence to a live photo of the user. Banking checks use information held by a financial institution, while digital ID services allow people to prove their age online without repeatedly showing physical documents.

Facial age estimation uses technology to analyse a user's face to estimate their age. Because this method has a margin of error, Ofcom suggests it should be used alongside a second check, similar to how shops use 'Challenge 25' when selling alcohol. Ofcom notes that some 16 and 17-year-olds may need to obtain a photo ID to use these methods.

Ofcom says that while some children may still find ways to bypass these checks, the measures should significantly reduce the likelihood of under-16s accessing restricted sites. The regulator will monitor these attempts and publish research within twelve months of the ban starting to help inform future government actions.

Am I affected?

If you are over 16, you may be asked to prove your age using one of the four methods listed.

What to do

  • Check if you have a valid passport or driving licence, as these are key for photo ID checks.
  • Consider setting up a digital ID service if you do not already have one, to make future age checks easier.
  • Keep your banking details up to date, as some platforms may use this to verify your age.
  • Be prepared to complete an age check when logging in to social media sites next year.
