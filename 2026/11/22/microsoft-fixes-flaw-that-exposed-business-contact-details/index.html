Microsoft fixes flaw that exposed business contact details

Microsoft has fixed a security flaw in its Entra Connect Health service that allowed attackers to view some business contact details.

Sunday 22 November 2026, 6.44pm

What happened

Microsoft says it found a weakness in its Entra Connect Health service on 9 November 2026. This service allows administrators to monitor the health, performance, and alerts of their on-premises identity infrastructure through the Microsoft Entra admin centre. Attackers used this weakness to look at certain information about companies.

The company confirmed that attackers could see email addresses for technical contacts and administrators. They could also see basic details like company names and country codes. Microsoft says it did not find any evidence that attackers changed any settings or stole passwords.

Microsoft fixed the problem on 19 November 2026. It has now told affected businesses about the issue. The company has not said how many businesses were affected or who the attackers were.

Microsoft advises businesses to be careful. It warns that scammers might use the exposed email addresses to send fake messages. These messages, known as phishing, try to trick people into giving away more details.

Am I affected?

This story affects businesses that use Microsoft Entra Connect Health. If you work for a company, check if your IT team has received a notification from Microsoft. The sources do not say if any individual home users were affected.

What to do

  • Check your email for any messages from Microsoft about this security issue.
  • Be extra careful with emails asking for passwords or money, as scammers may use the exposed contact details.
  • Make sure your work accounts use strong, multi-step security checks to log in.
  • Tell your IT team if you see any unusual activity in your work emails.
