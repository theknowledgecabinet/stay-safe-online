Chase, Visa and Mastercard confirm data breach at third-party firm

A cyberattack on a data analytics company may have exposed some customer names and email addresses, but no card numbers were stolen.

Sunday 22 November 2026, 8.07pm

What happened

J.P. Morgan Chase, Visa and Mastercard have confirmed that a cyberattack at a third-party service provider exposed some customer information. The bank said the incident did not disrupt its services and is unrelated to other recent outages.

The n0n ransomware group claims it stole data from Qubole, a cloud data analytics company. The group says it accessed customer files between 14 and 26 October 2026. It alleges it stole contracts, pricing records and staff information.

Visa stated that its systems were not involved and no payment data, such as card numbers, were affected. Mastercard said the exposure involved a limited amount of email addresses from a small subset of cardholders in a specific benefit program.

Both companies say their payment networks are working normally. Mastercard added that the exposure has been contained and they are working with the provider to support affected cardholders.

Am I affected?

The sources say the breach affected some Chase, Visa and Mastercard customers. Mastercard noted that the exposure involved a small subset of cardholders enrolled in a specific benefit program. You can check if you are affected by looking for emails or letters from your bank or card provider. You should also review your bank statements for any unusual activity.

What to do

  • Check your email and post for official notices from your bank or card provider.
  • Review your bank statements and card transactions for any unrecognised charges.
  • Be careful of phishing messages asking for your card details, as scammers may use this news to trick you.
  • Keep your online banking passwords unique and use two-factor authentication if available.
