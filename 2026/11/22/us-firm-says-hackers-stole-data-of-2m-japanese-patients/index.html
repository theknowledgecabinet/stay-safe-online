US firm says hackers stole data of 2m Japanese patients

A US medical services company says hackers stole personal and medical data from over two million people in Japan.

Sunday 22 November 2026, 8.08pm

What happened

SBC Medical Group Holdings, a US-based company that manages clinics, has disclosed a data breach. The company said hackers accessed an online platform it manages and stole data from databases linked to clinics in Japan.

According to a filing with the US Securities and Exchange Commission, the attackers took basic details such as names, addresses, and dates of birth for 1,624,000 people. They also stole medical treatment descriptions and review text for thousands of others. SBC Medical Group said 14 people had credit card details stolen, but the company confirmed these cards were expired and could not be used for payments.

The company also reported that customer passwords and employee records were compromised. In a separate incident, hackers accessed a third-party booking service called Tribeau, affecting around 19,000 people. SBC Medical Group said it has isolated the affected servers and changed passwords to stop the attackers.

The company is still investigating the full impact on its business. It has not said how the hackers got in or if the data has been shared publicly.

Am I affected?

The sources say the breach affects individuals associated with certain clinics in Japan. Check your bank statements and email for any unusual activity if you are unsure.

What to do

  • Change your passwords if you use the same one for other accounts.
  • Watch for phishing emails - fake messages that try to trick you into giving away details.
  • Check your bank statements for any unrecognised charges.
