Stranger danger is top worry for parents of online gamers

New Ofcom research shows talking to strangers is the biggest concern for parents, with over half of children playing with people they have never met.

Sunday 22 November 2026, 8.04pm

What happened

Ofcom has published a new report highlighting that 'stranger danger' is the primary worry for parents of children who game online. Almost two-thirds of parents are concerned about their child talking to strangers through games or chat functions.

This concern is well-founded, as more than half of children who game online say they play with people they have never met in person. One in five children do this all or most of the time they play. This worry ranks higher than concerns about bullying, inappropriate content, or pressure to buy items in games.

While most parents set rules about how long their children can play, fewer than half set rules about who they can interact with. Ofcom notes that gaming is now a daily part of childhood for almost nine in ten children aged three to 17.

To help families this half term, Ofcom is encouraging parents to use a simple three-step approach: Check, Chat, and Change. This involves reviewing game settings, talking openly with children about their gaming experiences, and making adjustments to safety features where needed.

Am I affected?

The sources say this affects parents of children aged 3 to 17 who play games online. You can check if your child is affected by looking at their gaming accounts to see who they are playing with and chatting to.

What to do

  • Check the settings on your child's gaming accounts, including their registered age and who they can be contacted by.
  • Chat regularly with your child about who they play with and if anything has made them feel uncomfortable.
  • Change settings to restrict contact from unknown players or switch on other safety features if needed.
