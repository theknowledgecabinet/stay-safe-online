New tactic: delivery photo upload failure to steal your parcel

Criminals are using a new trick to steal parcels by claiming a photo upload failed, leaving shoppers to fight for refunds.

Sunday 22 November 2026, 6.47pm

What happened

Reports of parcel theft in the UK have nearly doubled in a year. A Freedom of Information request by parcel firm Quadient found 12,802 reports in the year to June, up from 6,851 the previous year. The true number is likely higher because not all police forces provided data and many crimes go unreported.

Which? has heard reports of a new tactic. A delivery driver takes a picture of your doorstep as proof of delivery. They then claim the image cannot be uploaded due to a technical problem. They take the parcel back, promising to redeliver it later. In reality, the parcel is marked as delivered, making it hard to get a refund.

There are also reports of drivers taking a photo of a front door and driving off without getting out of the van. One man, Jon Barnes, reportedly found his Amazon locker empty. He took a photo as evidence, but Amazon initially refused a refund and threatened to close his account. He later received a reimbursement after contacting Which? and police.

Consumer law says the retailer must prove safe delivery. However, in practice, you may need to provide evidence that a parcel was missing. Staffed collection points, like post offices, offer a human witness, which can help if a parcel goes missing.

Am I affected?

The sources do not specify which retailers are most targeted, but the issue is widespread.

What to do

  • Do not nominate a 'safe place' for delivery, as you are liable if the parcel disappears.
  • If a parcel is missing, take a photo or video immediately and contact the retailer while you are still at the collection point.
  • Use a credit card for high-value purchases to get stronger consumer protection under Section 75.
  • Be wary of 'delivery' texts and emails, as these can be scams. Use the tracking link sent by the retailer instead.
