New rules force apps to fight child abuse images harder

Ofcom has announced stricter rules requiring online platforms to use better tools to find and remove child sexual abuse material.

Sunday 22 November 2026, 8.05pm

What happened

Ofcom, the UK’s online safety regulator, has set out new rules for websites and apps. These platforms must take stronger action to stop child sexual abuse material (CSAM) from being shared. The new measures come into force in early 2027, subject to parliamentary approval.

The rules require platforms to ban users who create or share this content. They must also use a method called 'hash matching' to find known illegal images. Hash matching is a way of identifying files by their digital fingerprint, so the same image can be spotted on different sites. This will be used on more sites, including pornography websites.

Ofcom also wants platforms to use automated tools to find new, previously unknown images. This will apply to file sharing services and the biggest social media platforms. The regulator says the scale of this problem is getting worse, partly because of artificial intelligence (AI) tools that can create fake images.

Oliver Griffiths, Ofcom’s Online Safety Group Director, said the current efforts by companies are not enough. He warned that firms that do not follow these new rules should expect to face serious consequences. The NSPCC, a children's charity, welcomed the move, noting that police data shows an average of 100 child sexual abuse image crimes happen in the UK every day.

Am I affected?

These rules apply to online platforms, not directly to individual users. If you use social media or file sharing services, these companies will have to update their systems to follow the new laws. The sources do not say who is affected.

What to do

  • Keep your accounts secure by using strong, unique passwords and two-factor authentication.
  • Report any illegal content you see on social media or file sharing sites using the platform's reporting tools.
  • Stay informed about online safety guidance from Ofcom and other UK organisations.
